Turned
Inside Out.

Three installations, three different confrontations with the body: a table set for a mind that consumes itself, an elevator lined with a hundred watching eyes, a sculpture that answers to a live heartbeat.

Each piece asks a viewer to stop being only a viewer.

PROJECT.01

Silent Feast

Medium: 3D Modeling, mixed media — metal, glass, mirror

Silent Feast is about my struggle with anxiety and how I used food to cope — the self-destructive mechanics of binge eating, staged as a place setting. A narrow, metallic table with a glass top holds a bowl of silver-painted baby doll heads, each a different size, each a stage of myself. Beside them, a candle wears another head, and a metallic brain sits between a spoon and fork. Look down through the glass and a mirror underneath reveals the brain's secret: its underside is a mouth full of teeth, silently chewing through the heads. The mind consuming itself in a silent, endless cycle.

The piece blurs the line between nourishment and punishment. The mirror forces viewers to confront their own complicity in the feast, implicating them in the voyeurism of self-destruction. The table feels sterile and cold, like a hospital or a lab; the doll heads are emotionless and helpless, and the brain keeps consuming them. By framing the brain as both the source of pain and the instrument of its own consumption, the piece mirrors the cyclical nature of anxiety, where seeking relief only perpetuates the suffering.

PROJECT.02

The Observer

Medium: Photography, print, public elevator

A site-specific installation of roughly 100 fisheye photographs of my own eye, taped in four horizontal rows at eye level inside an elevator in the art building. The elevator — a space that is transitional both physically and psychologically — becomes the stage for an exploration of perception and introspection.

The work stages a collapse between observer and observed. We're conditioned to watch others constantly, especially in social and academic spaces, but rarely notice how obsessively we watch ourselves — through mirrors, screens, surveillance, memory. Only the eyes showing visible tears or redness carry a legible emotional narrative; every other expression is rendered ambiguous, which mirrors my own emotional state while making the photographs.

The elevator is intentional: enclosed, intimate, often awkward, and encountered involuntarily by people who are simply trying to get somewhere, not visiting a gallery. Interrupted by a multiplicity of gazes, they become aware of themselves — their expressions, their bodies, their proximity to strangers. The piece doesn't offer clarity. It offers tension, a quiet confrontation.

PROJECT.03

Signal Fifty-Five

Medium: Electronic tech / art — ESP32-S3, pulse sensor, LED, fabric

What does it mean to make the body's most involuntary signal visible? The heartbeat is always there — constant, hidden, unnoticed. This sculpture borrows it and gives it form. What you see in the light is not a simulation of life. It is yours.

Built around an ESP32-S3 dev board, a MAX30102 pulse oximeter sensor, and a WS2812B addressable LED strip inside a semi-transparent fabric form, the sculpture reads a live heartbeat from whoever holds the sensor and answers in light. Making it meant working close to the hardware: soldering the board, sensor, and LED strip together by hand, then writing the firmware in the Arduino IDE — reading the pulse signal, smoothing it into a stable BPM, and mapping that to the lighting behavior the sculpture shows.

  1. Rest your fingertip gently on the small sensor
  2. Hold still and breathe naturally
  3. Watch the sculpture respond to your body
  4. The light is yours — it belongs to no one else
