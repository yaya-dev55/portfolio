Structure,
Worn.

3D-printed wearable sculpture exploring the boundary between body, ornament, and structure.

Structure, Worn treats the body as a site of construction rather than simply a surface to decorate. Designed digitally and realized as a physical wearable object, the bangle extends the geometry of the wrist into an unfamiliar form — part ornament, part architecture, part external anatomy.

Rather than following the traditional language of jewelry, the piece asks what happens when adornment becomes structural. Its exaggerated form changes the silhouette of the body and creates a subtle tension between something meant to be worn and something that appears to belong to the body itself.

The work explores the space between natural and constructed, intimate and unfamiliar, functional and sculptural. By placing an architectural form directly onto the body, it turns an everyday gesture of adornment into an encounter with an alternate version of the self.

04 — DIGITAL SCULPTURE

Untitled Designs

DESIGN.01

Untitled

Medium: Digital sculpture / render

A hybrid creature — part feline, part insect — draped over a black sphere and suspended in an electric blue field. The body reads as skin and shell at once: cracked, plated, tendrilled at the face.

Grey orbs drift around it and a stack of angular shards rises underneath like a plinth that never settles. An early design in staging a sculpted figure inside a saturated, weightless space.

DESIGN.02

Untitled

Medium: Digital sculpture / render

Bust portraits of a sphynx-like figure with tall pointed ears and a face carved in fine, symmetrical linework. The skin is scaled; the torso carries an ornamental border low on the belly.

Glass and shrapnel erupt from the shoulders where a garment would be, set against a deep oxblood ground. The same head as the design above, brought forward and lit as a character design.

DESIGN.03

Untitled

Medium: Digital sculpture / render

A looser, abstract composition — fragments floating on a neutral grey ground. A red, veined mass with a heart-like density hangs at the center, ringed by crystalline glass shards and pale, ashen clusters.

No figure, no plinth: just matter caught mid-break, tested for how material and color hold weight on their own.
